    Mitigation Factors

    Crowd Safety Reference

    Some general tips on preventing a riot from breaking out at a sporting event, and what to do if one actually happens. This isn't tied to any specific team or venue, just stuff that should apply pretty much anywhere.

    Before a Riot

    Prevention

    Venue & Security Planning

    • Conduct a risk assessment for every fixture based on rivalry history, match stakes, and expected attendance
    • Enforce ticketed capacity strictly; never allow attendance to exceed a venue's certified safe limit
    • Physically segregate opposing supporter sections with adequate barriers and neutral buffer zones
    • Maintain multiple, clearly marked, unobstructed emergency exits and evacuation routes
    • Deploy trained stewards and police at a ratio appropriate to the match's assessed risk level

    Fan & Ticketing Controls

    • Use identity-verified ticketing to keep individuals with prior stadium bans out of the venue
    • Restrict or suspend alcohol sales in the hours immediately before kickoff and in high-risk sections
    • Coordinate visiting supporters' travel and arrival timing with local police to avoid chance confrontations
    • Maintain dialogue with organized supporter groups through dedicated fan liaison officers

    Intelligence & Coordination

    • Share intelligence between clubs, leagues, and police on rivalry history and known risk indicators
    • Hold joint pre-match briefings with security, stewarding, and medical teams
    • Monitor public channels for signs of planned confrontations or unusually high tension beforehand

    Facility Design

    • Ensure adequate lighting, signage, and CCTV coverage throughout the venue and surrounding areas
    • Design entry and exit points to prevent bottlenecks that can turn into crowd crushes
    • Restrict access to items that are easily used as projectiles

    If a Riot Occurs

    Response

    Immediate Crowd Control

    • Prioritize de-escalation and a proportionate response over aggressive dispersal tactics
    • Avoid firing tear gas or similar agents into enclosed or narrow spaces where a stampede could follow
    • Open emergency exits immediately and direct crowds calmly and clearly toward them

    Communication

    • Use the stadium PA system and video screens to issue clear, calm, repeated instructions
    • Establish a single incident command structure so police, stewards, and medical teams aren't giving conflicting directions
    • Avoid alarmist or contradictory announcements, which can trigger panic on their own

    Medical & Safety Response

    • Pre-position medical teams and ambulances to reach any part of the venue quickly
    • Establish triage points at a safe distance from the immediate danger zone
    • Account for all players, staff, and officials before declaring an evacuation complete

    Post-Incident Actions

    • Preserve CCTV footage and witness accounts for an independent investigation
    • Commission a transparent post-incident review that identifies root causes, not just individuals to blame
    • Communicate openly with the public and with affected families
    • Follow through on the structural or policy changes a review recommends, not only short-term sanctions

    About Me

    Rohan Satpathy, Developer

    CrowdPulse is my project for looking at how crowd violence at sporting events actually happens. It maps 20 real riots, uses AI to summarize each one and answer questions about it, and can size up the risk for an upcoming match. There's also a data dashboard where you can upload your own spreadsheet and see the numbers as charts. It's built with plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with Leaflet and OpenStreetMap for the map and the Gemini API behind the AI parts.
